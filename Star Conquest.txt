Here's what I could dig up on Strategic Commander by ZindaWare (Palm OS, ~2001–2003, versions up to 1.2). It's mostly abandonware with no surviving official manual online, but between store descriptions, the developer's own feature list, and a contemporary player review, the core rules are fairly clear. I also found that someone already built an open-source spiritual clone (Fleet Commander, Kotlin/AngularJS) explicitly inspired by it — confirms the genre reading.
Confirmed facts about the original game
Genre / comparisons the publisher itself used: turn-based 4X-lite strategy, explicitly compared by ZindaWare to Master of Orion, Risk, and Civilization — i.e. "explore, expand, exploit, exterminate" in miniature.
Setup
Set in space; the map is a starfield of planets.
4 universe (map) sizes to choose from.
Up to 9 players total, any mix of human and AI (up to 8 opponents).
3 AI difficulty levels.
Each game generates a different random universe layout.
Each player starts on their own random home planet.
Supports B&W, color, and hi-res color Palm screens; later versions (1.2) added infrared "beaming" so multiple physical Palm devices could pass turns to each other, plus multi-game save slots.
Core loop (turn-based)
On planets you control, you build manufacturing facilities and research facilities (a planet has limited building slots, and different planet "classes" support more or fewer facilities).
Each turn you allocate what your facilities do — the reviewer describes it as choosing a mix of: produce gold/resources, research tech, or build ships. It's an investment trade-off: rush research for a small elite high-tech fleet, or rush production for a massive low-tech fleet, or balance both.
Gold/resources + tech level let you construct ships and send them out.
You move fleets between planets by tap-and-drag on the map (this wasn't explained in-game — a common complaint).
Ships explore neutral planets and can colonize/conquer them, expanding your building capacity and resource base.
When a fleet arrives at an enemy-held planet, an automatic battle resolves — no direct player control during combat. It's shown as a numeric fleet-strength comparison plus a simple animation (torpedoes flying, ships disappearing). Outcome is driven by fleet size and tech level of ships — a huge low-tech fleet can still lose to a handful of high-tech ships.
Win condition: capture every other player's/AI's home planet. Do that to all opponents and you win. (Losing your own home planet presumably eliminates or badly cripples you, though this isn't explicitly documented.)
What's notably NOT documented anywhere I could find (soft32/Tucows/PalmDB/GameFAQs/archive.org all lack a rules manual, and the Wayback Machine copy of the original zindaware.com site is blocked from fetching):
Exact resource types/units (is it just "gold," or gold + separate research points?)
Specific ship types/classes and their stats
Specific building types, costs, and build times
The exact tech tree / research levels
Exact combat formula (how fleet size and tech translate into win probability/losses)
Planet "classes" and how many facility slots each supports
Whether facilities produce passively each turn or need to be manually "activated"
Since none of that survives publicly, a faithful byte-for-byte clone isn't really possible — but a design-accurate clone of the systems and feel absolutely is, and that's normal for this kind of retro-clone project (this is exactly what the Fleet Commander dev did too).
Suggested rule set for your clone (fills the documented gaps with clean, tunable numbers)
If you want, I can write this up as a fuller game design doc and then start building the actual JavaScript/HTML/CSS implementation (canvas or SVG map, drag-to-send-fleet interaction, turn-based state machine, simple AI). A reasonable, faithful-in-spirit rule set:
Map: N planets scattered on a 2D starfield, size scales with your 4 "universe size" presets (e.g. Tiny/Small/Medium/Large → 12/24/40/60 planets).
Planets: each has a class (I–V) determining max facility slots (e.g. 2/4/6/8/10) and a neutral defense garrison if unowned.
Facilities: two types — Factory (converts turns into ship-building capacity/resources) and Lab (converts turns into tech-level progress). Each facility built adds to your per-turn output.
Resources: a single currency ("Credits") produced by factories, spent to build more facilities or ships.
Tech level: raised by labs; higher tech unlocks stronger ship classes and gives a combat multiplier.
Ships: 3–4 tiers (Scout → Fighter → Cruiser → Dreadnought), each costing more credits + minimum tech level, with a base attack/defense stat.
Movement: drag from a friendly planet to a destination; fleet takes N turns based on distance/speed stat.
Combat: auto-resolve on arrival — total (ship count × ship power × tech multiplier) attacker vs. defender, with some randomness; loser fleet destroyed, if attacker wins and target was a planet, ownership flips.
Turn structure: simultaneous turns (all players queue orders, then resolve) is more faithful to "turn-based, not real-time" and works well for a mobile/web clone with AI opponents.
Win condition: capture all rivals' home planets.
